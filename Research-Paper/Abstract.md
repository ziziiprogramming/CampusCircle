This paper presents CampusCircle, a full-stack web application designed to address a recurring and measurable problem in engineering colleges across India: first-year students spend between ₹3,000 and ₹6,000 in the first two weeks of their academic life on materials, drafting instruments, textbooks, lab coats, and scientific calculators, that their seniors have already purchased and no longer use. The platform operationalises a borrow-first philosophy, connecting verified students within the same institution through a structured, trust-rated lending and knowledge-sharing system. This paper documents the problem identification process, original survey-based research and validation, product design decisions, technical architecture, challenges encountered, and projected impact. It is written from the perspective of the founding student developer and is intended both as an academic submission and as a publicly shareable founder's document.
Keywords: peer-to-peer lending, campus marketplace, student sustainability, full-stack development, borrow economy, trust systems, engineering education
